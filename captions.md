# Captions and accessible descriptions

Personal story. Not official Microsoft guidance or endorsement.

## role-evolution.svg

**Caption:** Use AI, build a workflow, share it. Guardrails apply at every stage, and infrastructure expertise stays the foundation.

**Accessible description:** A top-to-bottom story that starts in 2013 with a move from on-premises to cloud-first work: I made a change like this before. Three stages follow inside a dashed frame. Stage 1, Use, May 2025: first useful results with AI. Lesson: start with one repeated task. Stage 2, Build, late 2025 to 2026: APEX, where AI prepares and humans decide. Lesson: turn repeated work into a workflow. Stage 3, Scale, the past nine months: webinars and bootcamps, with others delivering it too. Lesson: share it. The frame lists two guardrails for all stages: verify every output, and bring in specialists for decisions beyond my expertise. A dark band beneath says infrastructure expertise stays the foundation at every stage. A closing note says AI widens the work I take on and does not make me an expert in every specialty.

## human-ai-specialist-workflow.svg

**Caption:** AI assists, but checking, specialist input when needed, and production approval are human steps.

**Accessible description:** Six steps in order, top to bottom. 1, me: discover the business problem, why it needs solving, and how success is measured. 2, me: develop the technical architecture. 3, AI-assisted: use AI to assist with analysis, code and iteration. 4, me, human check: check outputs against requirements, Azure Policy constraints and available evidence. 5, specialist, when needed: for decisions beyond my expertise, consult an experienced application or data specialist. 6, qualified people: production approval stays with suitably qualified people, and I do not approve applications for production on my own. Border styles (solid, dashed, double) and text tags mark who does each step. A note says passing builds and tests does not replace specialist judgment.

## apex-engagement-timeline.svg

**Caption:** One anonymized customer engagement with three separate timings: under 18 hours elapsed to completed Terraform, about six hands-on hours, and a successful deployment on day three. Customer-reported experience, not a controlled benchmark.

**Accessible description:** Four steps. 1, Azure discovery and a workload diagram, compared with the customer's diagram: it matched. 2, a Well-Architected assessment compared with Azure Advisor: every relevant finding I checked appeared in the assessment. 3, assessment of the existing Terraform and synthesis of workload requirements, including SLA, RTO, RPO and compliance. 4, updated Azure Verified Modules-based Terraform for another region. Three separate timings follow: under 18 hours elapsed to completed Terraform, including AI processing and waiting; about 6 hours of my hands-on time; and a successful deployment on day three, a separate, later milestone that is not part of the 18 hours. A note summarizes customer-reported feedback: the customer told me that what we accomplished together in three days was something they had been trying to do for about six months. They also said the engagement showed them how they could use AI more efficiently. It adds that this is customer-reported experience from one anonymized engagement, not a controlled benchmark or proof of universal accuracy.

## Astro usage note

Each SVG uses `width="100%"` and a 420-unit `viewBox`, so it scales to its container. Inline it, or use `<img>` with the alt text from the descriptions above.

All text is 16.5 units or larger (tags, body and notes), and headings are 19 to 22 units. Rendered at a 360px content width this is about 14px or larger (16.5 × 360/420 ≈ 14.1px). At a 320px content width it is about 12.6px, which is readable but below the 14px target. For the best legibility on very small screens, give the SVGs at least 360px of content width. No text was clipped or overlapping at 360px or 320px.
